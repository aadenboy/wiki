Cag

From the Aadenboy Wiki
Jump to navigation Jump to search

Cags, also referred to as generic aadenboy cats, are a category of anthropomorphized cats which possess a certain set of features. Importantly, it is incorrect to treat them as a species, as common properties are often bent in unique ways.

Ontology

The minimal set of features common between cags are a head and a torso, with the head consisting of at minimum eyes and ears, though a mouth is also common. Cags are most often monopedal, though recurring characters tend to have a higher pedality. Their internal anatomy is more closely modeled after humans rather than house cats, and some features of their anatomy are stylized and simplified, usually composing of only basic primitives like cylinders and spheres—the head in particular often has no snout.

For cags which have paws, it is most common for the paw to be portrayed as a simple sphere with or without a thumb, and can be given three or four digits if necessary. The paws do not have any paw pads. The front arms of quadrupeds have greater mobility and dexterity compared to a regular house cat, however this isn't present in cags which already have a pair of dedicated arms. All cags are plantigrade, making their legs cylinders and bend forwards alike a human's. Their fur can be described as smooth and silky, and is usually a solid color throughout the body.

All cags are capable of speech, and have the same diet and vision as humans. The eyes of a cag, while usually ambiguous, are like that of a human's. The mouth also shares similar properties to humans in addition to pronounced fangs.

The actual features present in each cag is highly diverse, and any one cag can be wildly different from the norm. Common additions to distinguish cags are tufts of fur which protrude from the cheeks or forehead, whiskers (usually three per side), the shape of the tail, and/or the design of the eyes.
